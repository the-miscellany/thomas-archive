*The Thomas Book* is where the family's Welsh ancestry comes from, but nothing in it connects our line to the Welsh Thomases: the link rests on the family's use of the same coat of arms. Its appendix abstracts records for many men named John Thomas, and two of them may be the immigrant. The post on [the first Thomases in Virginia](/early-thomas-line/) sets out what each source claims.

## Transcriptions

From p. 572:

> THOMAS, JOHN, of Isle of Wight Co., Va., or that section of the State, had a son, John, who removed to North Carolina and *m.* Mollie Edwards, nearly related to the Hon. Wedon Edwards, of Franklin Co., N. C., and had a son, Jordan, *m.* Ann, *dau.* of Gabriel and Sarah (Richmond) Long, and *granddau.* of Colonel Nicholas Long, of Quankey, Halifax Co., N. C., and Rebecca Hill, of Virginia. They had a son, William George, of Wilmington, N. C., living in 1876. Authority: Family papers, through Mr. William G. Thomas.

From p. 589:

> THOMAS, JOHN, No. 2, p. 158. 350 acres on the north side of Queen's Creek in York Co.; west of lands of Joseph Croshaw; 300 acres formerly granted to John Broach and assigned 17th 8ber, 1649. 50 acres for wife Dorothy.

> THOMAS, JOHN, No. 3, p. 300. 174 acres in "Nanzemon" Co., on the east side of Newtowne Haven River, near his own land and land of Mr. Jordan and Thos. Best; 150 acres formerly granted to Peter Mountague, December 8, 1645, and assigned; 24 acres for one transport, November 19, 1654.
