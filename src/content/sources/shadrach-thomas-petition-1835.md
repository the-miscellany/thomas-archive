Under Mexican law, a settler who wanted land in one of the colonies (*empresas*) had to petition the colony's commissioner, with a certificate of good character. This is Shadrach's, written in Spanish and signed by him at Nacogdoches a year before Texas independence.

The transcription and translation below are a first draft, read from the scan. A few words are uncertain.

## Transcription

> No. 319. Sor. Comisionado Especial de la Empresa de Js. Vehlein.
>
> Shadrach D. Thomas, natural de los Estados Unidos del norte, con el debido respeto ante V. me presento y digo: que, atraído por las generosas disposiciones de las leyes de colonización de este Estado, he venido con mi muger y cuatro hijos a radicarme en él; si en vista de la adjunta certificación V. tuviere a bien admitirme como colono en la referida Empresa, concediéndome la cantidad de tierra que me corresponde en los baldíos de la misma. Por tanto:
>
> A V. suplico disponga como pido, que en ello recibiré gracia y justicia. Nacogdoches, Mayo 1 de 1835.
>
> Shadrach D. Thomas

## Translation

> No. 319. To the Special Commissioner of Joseph Vehlein's Colony.
>
> Shadrach D. Thomas, a native of the United States of the North, with due respect presents himself before you and says: that, drawn by the generous provisions of the colonization laws of this State, he has come with his wife and four children to settle in it; and that, in view of the attached certificate, you may see fit to admit him as a colonist of the said colony, granting him the amount of land due to him from its vacant lands. Therefore:
>
> He begs you to grant his request, for which he will be grateful. Nacogdoches, 1 May 1835.
>
> Shadrach D. Thomas
